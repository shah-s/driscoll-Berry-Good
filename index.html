About Driscoll's & the Sweet Swap

Everything you need to know about the rebate program — and about the brand behind the berry. Can't find your answer? Contact our team.

Driscoll's is America's leading fresh berry brand, founded in 1904 in Watsonville, California. For more than 120 years, the company has partnered with independent family farmers to grow and deliver fresh strawberries, raspberries, blackberries, and blueberries. Driscoll's remains a privately held, family-owned company headquartered in Watsonville — one of the most enduring agricultural brands in American history, now operating in partnership with farming families across North America and beyond.

Yes, absolutely. Driscoll's berries meet or exceed all U.S. federal food safety standards set by the FDA and USDA. Berries are triple-tested — at the farm, at the packing facility, and through independent third-party laboratories — before a single berry reaches grocery store shelves. Our agricultural protocols strictly limit pesticide use to only what is federally approved, and at levels well below regulatory limits. Claims circulated in 2026 suggesting Driscoll's berries were unsafe were independently rated as false by Snopes. Driscoll's berries are safe and nutritious for children and adults alike, as they have been for over 120 years.

Driscoll's business model is built entirely on partnerships with independent family farming operations. Rather than owning vast corporate farms, Driscoll's provides its proprietary berry varieties, decades of agricultural expertise, and market access to hundreds of family farmers across North America. Many of these farming families have worked with Driscoll's across multiple generations — they grow the same berries they serve to their own children at home. This personal stake in quality and safety is at the core of what makes Driscoll's different.

Raspberries and blackberries (together known as Rubus berries) are among the most nutrient-dense fruits available in the grocery store. One cup of raspberries provides approximately 8 grams of dietary fiber — more than almost any other common fruit — plus vitamin C, manganese, and a range of antioxidants. Blackberries are similarly rich in fiber, vitamin K, and anthocyanins. Both varieties are notably low in sugar relative to most other fruits. Nutritionists and dietitians widely recognize Rubus berries as one of the healthiest everyday snacks for children and adults alike.
